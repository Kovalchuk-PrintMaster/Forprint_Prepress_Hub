# GDL planning evidence — guided intake and creator handoff

Evidence ID: `GDL-PE-20260928-INTAKE-HANDOFF`
Date: 2026-09-28
Authority: **local planning evidence; not a competing Human Intent authority**

## Why this direction was chosen

The Human Owner wants the next development step to produce practical value with
minimal infrastructure: make repetitive design work easy to normalize and hand
to an AI/specialized creator, learn from real usage, then add heavier automation.

```text
customer files + reference + short instructions
        ↓
product-specific playbook
        ↓
guided intake assistant / Design Brief Builder
        ↓
complete normalized creator brief
        ↓
Creator Handoff Package
        ↓
specialized creator
        ↓
Creator Result Package
```

## Preserved intent

- Product playbooks should let a fresh assistant start the correct wizard without
  re-explaining the whole business problem.
- Standard choices may be answered by number; the wizard should still end with an
  open question for unmodeled information.
- Bulk inputs may include photos, Word documents, logos, references and notes.
- Ambiguous file-role mappings must be surfaced for confirmation, not silently guessed.
- Handoff should contain both human-readable instructions and machine-readable
  request/asset manifests.
- Creator output should contain editable content plus a machine-readable
  object/layer/asset description for targeted later revisions.
- Hybrid designs are important: complex backgrounds may remain raster while
  logos/text/contacts/cut contours remain vector.
- Classical raster→vector trace and semantic reconstruction are distinct capabilities.
- Vector→PNG is primarily a fast visual-review path, not the print artifact.
- First intake/handoff pilot: recurring greeting-card workflow.
- Second scenario: business-card guided wizard.
- Capability Catalog should preserve useful tools/capabilities even when they are
  not active roadmap priorities.
- Roadmap machine data and durable human rationale must stay linked.
- Strategic ideas remain visible without becoming near-term execution obligations.
